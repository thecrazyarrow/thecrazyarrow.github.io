Custom Cement 3D Printer

My major college engineering project: a cement-based 3D printer designed and built from scratch, covering the mechanical structure, motion, electronics and extrusion.

Details

Problem

Cement behaves nothing like thermoplastic. Material consistency, flow rate, extrusion pressure, nozzle geometry and layer deposition all had to be solved, and motion and material flow had to work as one system.

Process

A BigTreeTech controller with stepper motors and drivers, a custom auger-based extruder and a peristaltic pump for material delivery, running Marlin firmware. Designed in FreeCAD and refined through repeated build, test and fix cycles.

Result

A complete printer, built by hand, where the motion system and the cement flow work together to lay down layers.

  2. Mechanical design
  3. Electronics
  4. Fabrication
  5. Assembly
  6. Control
  7. Testing
  8. Iteration

Universal Mouldable Splint

A splint printed flat on a standard printer, then heat-formed with hot water to fit the patient's limb.

Details

Problem

The idea came from a hospital visit. Casts are heavy and bulky, and one-size-fits-all splints rarely fit well.

Process

A flat-bed-printable design that softens in hot water and is shaped directly on the limb. Explored in collaboration with Athira Hospital, Chavara, Kollam.

Result

A splint that is lighter and less bulky than a cast, with adaptable geometry, designed for low-cost local fabrication.

  • FDM printing
  • Thermoforming
  • Product design

A product-development concept, not a clinically validated medical device.

3D-Printed Electric Guitar

In design

An original sci-fi, asymmetric, angular electric guitar designed in Autodesk Fusion.

Details

Problem

A full guitar body is far bigger than a hobby print bed, and it has to survive string tension.

Process

A 20 × 40 mm aluminium T-slot extrusion runs from the body into the neck as the backbone. The body has hexagonal through-holes and is split into bolt-together modules that fit a 220 × 220 × 250 mm bed.

Gesture-Controlled Robotic Arm

Built single-handedly: a robotic arm that follows hand gestures through computer vision.

Details

Problem

Control a robotic arm naturally, with your hand, without joysticks or a remote.

Process

MediaPipe tracks 21 points on the hand, YOLOv8 handles detection, and an Arduino drives the motors.

Result

An arm that responds to hand movements in real time.

  • MediaPipe
  • YOLOv8
  • Python
  • Arduino
  • 21-point hand tracking
